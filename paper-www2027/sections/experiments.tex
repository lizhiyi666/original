\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}
We test constraint satisfaction, distributional fidelity, and the contributions
of projection, decoding, and geometry refinement.

\subsection{Setup}
\label{sec:exp:setup}
\paragraph{Datasets.}
New York has $3{,}160$ training and $2{,}108$ test sequences; Istanbul has
$7{,}035$ and $4{,}914$. New York's sequence-level split reverses category-
precedence preferences, but does not guarantee disjoint complete partial-order
configurations. Istanbul uses the supplied historical OOD split, whose original
construction provenance is not fully recoverable. Each test reference supplies
an order matrix from actual category first/last positions (\S\ref{sec:problem}).
Sampling-time methods share frozen city-specific backbones; PO-CFG is retrained.

\paragraph{Protocol.}
Order, existence, token-KL, and distance-KL weights are $(1,5,1,1)$.
Projection uses Gumbel temperature $3$ every fourth step in the final $40$
reverse steps: $10$ outer by $50$ inner iterations (5,000 updates per active
batch), SGD rate $1$, gradient clip $10$, and no early stopping. Distance KL
uses $8$ routes, top-$32$ candidates and bins, and path temperature $1$.
Formal sampling uses $64$-sequence FP32 batches with TF32 disabled. The full
stack uses $(\rho,\lambda,\beta)=(0.5,0,1)$. Reported spreads are sample SDs
across seeds $135398$, $135399$, $135400$ at fixed checkpoints, not training
variability or confidence intervals.

\paragraph{Baselines.}
\textbf{JointGen} is the unconstrained backbone.
\textbf{PostSwap} stably reorders generated events according to $\Mmat$.
\textbf{EnergyGuide} takes one gradient step on order plus five times existence
energy per controlled step, without projection's KL anchor or dual updates.
\textbf{PO-CFG} is trained with partial-order conditioning for $1000$ epochs and
evaluated at classifier-free guidance scale $1$; it is the only retrained
baseline. Our variants are \pcdgp (projection), \pcdgpd (projection and
category-consistent decoding), and \pcdg (the full stack).

\paragraph{Metrics.}
\label{sec:exp:metrics}
All tables use the archived evaluation-v2 protocol.
Fidelity uses six JSDs: cumulative travel \emph{Distance}, \emph{Radius},
conditional \emph{CategoryTransition}, distinct daily POI count
(\emph{DailyLoc}), hourly \emph{Category} usage, and identity-aligned top-$1000$
POI visitation (\emph{G-RANK}). Distance is conditional on generated/reference
first and last categories matching; the eligible subset can differ by method.
Radius is the square root of mean distance to the centroid, not standard radius
of gyration. Appendix~\ref{app:metrics} specifies the fidelity statistics and filtering.

Constraint metrics use the catalog categories $\mathrm{cat}(l)$ of emitted
POIs, not the sampled category tokens. For reference sequence $i$, let $R_i$
contain each distinct-category pair $(a,b)$ satisfying
$\max\operatorname{pos}_i(a)<\min\operatorname{pos}_i(b)$.
Let $P_i\subseteq R_i$ contain pairs whose two categories both occur in the
generated sequence; $C_i$ is the set of categories involved in $R_i$ and
$\widehat C_i$ the generated category set. For a present pair, $v_i(a,b)$ is
the number of inverted occurrence pairs divided by $n_a n_b$.

Strict OVR averages $v_i$ over $R_i$, assigning $1$ to missing-category pairs,
then averages over sequences with nonempty $R_i$. Skip OVR averages $v_i$
over $P_i$, then over sequences with nonempty $P_i$. Coverage and Unsat pool counts:
\begin{align}
 \mathrm{PairCov}&=\frac{\sum_i|P_i|}{\sum_i|R_i|},\\
 \mathrm{CatCov}&=\frac{\sum_i|C_i\cap\widehat C_i|}{\sum_i|C_i|},\\
 \mathrm{Unsat}&=\frac{\sum_i\sum_{(a,b)\in R_i}
 \mathbf{1}[(a,b)\notin P_i\ \lor\ v_i(a,b)>0]}{\sum_i|R_i|}.
\end{align}
Unsat counts unsatisfied relations, not sequences; pair coverage requires
both categories to occur, not correct order. Empty denominators are undefined,
not zero; undefined OVR sequences are omitted from their respective means.
Tables express these rates in percent.

\subsection{Main results}
\label{sec:exp:main}
Table~\ref{tab:method} compares all methods on both cities.

\paragraph{Constraint satisfaction.}
The full \pcdg reduces strict OVR from $92.89\%$ to $4.04\%$ on Istanbul and
from $89.45\%$ to $11.28\%$ on New York, reductions of $88.85$ and $78.17$
percentage points. Unsat falls from $95.57\%$ to $7.29\%$ and from $93.64\%$
to $15.86\%$, while pair coverage increases by $6.5\times$ and $4.2\times$.
All five constraint metrics improve over PO-CFG in both cities.
PostSwap reaches zero skip OVR but leaves coverage unchanged, illustrating why
skip OVR alone is insufficient. EnergyGuide gives much smaller gains.

\paragraph{Fidelity and residuals.}
On Istanbul, projection alone degrades Distance ($0.1128\to0.1314$) and Radius
($0.0226\to0.0516$), while the full stack lowers all six JSDs below JointGen.
It also lowers five of six below PO-CFG; its G-RANK remains slightly higher
($0.1798$ vs.\ $0.1794$). On New York, four of six JSDs improve over JointGen.
CategoryTransition ($0.0070\to0.0183$) and G-RANK ($0.1612\to0.1914$) remain
higher, and PO-CFG is best on those two and Category.
The observed category-transition gap does not establish that such loss is
unavoidable for partial-order control. These comparisons use three-seed means;
no significance test is performed.

\subsection{Ablation of the projection stage}
\label{sec:exp:ablation}
Appendix~\ref{app:ablation} reports eight projection-stage variants:
\textsc{No Projection}, \textsc{No Existence}, \textsc{No Order},
\textsc{No KL} (both KL terms off), \textsc{No Distance KL},
\textsc{Fixed Multipliers}, \textsc{No Gumbel}, and \textsc{Full} ($=\pcdgp$).

Removing existence reduces pair coverage to $7.34\%$ on Istanbul and $10.95\%$
on New York, below JointGen's $14.63\%$ and $21.13\%$. Skip OVR also decreases,
to $7.67\%$ and $18.04\%$: omitting constrained categories evades the order
penalty. Removing order retains high coverage ($72.53\%$/$69.92\%$) but raises
strict OVR to $63.50\%$/$65.43\%$. Fixed multipliers increase strict OVR by
$9.90$/$19.94$ percentage points, supporting adaptive dual updates at this budget.
Distance KL lowers Distance and Radius in both cities: from $0.1441/0.0582$ to
$0.1314/0.0516$ on Istanbul and from $0.2484/0.1430$ to $0.2210/0.1265$ on New
York. Comparing \textsc{No KL} with \textsc{No Distance KL} isolates the token
anchor only with distance regularization disabled; it does not establish its
marginal effect in the full projection. Removing Gumbel noise has nonuniform
effects and lowers New York Distance to $0.2055$.

\subsection{Decomposing the fidelity--constraint trade-off}
\label{sec:exp:tradeoff}
Figure~\ref{fig:tradeoff} shows the Unsat--Distance plane; the complete stage-wise
means are in Table~\ref{tab:stagewise}, Appendix~\ref{app:ablation}.

\paragraph{Projection and decoding.}
Decoding (b)$\to$(c) reduces strict OVR from $32.80\%$ to $3.72\%$ on Istanbul
and from $38.51\%$ to $9.52\%$ on New York. Radius increases from $0.0516$ to
$0.0892$ and from $0.1265$ to $0.1681$, respectively. The fidelity changes are
metric-dependent: category-transition JSD also increases in both cities.
Thus coupling the token and catalog representations strengthens realized
activity-order control, but also changes the distribution of available POIs.

\paragraph{Geometry and distribution correction.}
At (c)$\to$(d), geometry preserves constraint metrics within $10^{-12}$.
Distance/Radius fall from $0.1539/0.0892$ to $0.0372/0.0159$ on Istanbul and
from $0.1989/0.1681$ to $0.0610/0.0382$ on New York.
Because actual categories are unchanged, Distance's endpoint filter is also
unchanged in this comparison. This isolates the spatial refinement effect under
fixed realized activities and a fixed Distance evaluation subset.
New York G-RANK nevertheless rises from $0.1914$ to $0.2069$.
Stage (e) lowers it to $0.1914$ and lowers Istanbul G-RANK from $0.1835$ to
$0.1798$, with a modest constraint regression. This transition jointly changes
$\rho$, $\beta$, and the candidate pool, so it does not isolate their individual
effects. Selection of $\lambda=0$ identifies a preferred configuration within
the evaluated grid, not evidence that category-side fidelity loss is absent.

\paragraph{Selection protocol.}
The $18$ configurations were screened on training-side calibration records
already seen by the backbone, not an independent validation set.
Ranking minimizes the maximum, then mean, of twelve city--metric JSD/JointGen
ratios. The two shortlisted configurations underwent three-seed confirmation;
neither passed all distribution gates. The final candidate was chosen from
them while retaining the constraint gates, with an explicit override of the
failed distribution gates, and evaluated on the test set without reopening
selection. Final ranking used v3 re-evaluation; both screening order and final
candidate order agree with v2 (Appendix~\ref{app:grid}).
No test metric entered hyperparameter selection, candidate construction, or
the refinement objective.

\subsection{Cost and reproducibility}
\label{sec:exp:cost}
\pcdg requires no retraining but adds projection, decoding, and refinement work.
In the earlier geometry qualification, the selected $200$-step refinement
increased median spatial-sampling time by $3.35$--$3.98\%$ relative to projection
Full, not JointGen. These measurements do not establish overhead for the later
full stack. Comparable full-stack/JointGen timing and PO-CFG training GPU-hours
are not measured in the archived cost evidence.
Appendix~\ref{app:cost} gives measured batch times and their scope.
