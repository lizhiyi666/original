\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Location-based social networks (LBSNs) produce time-stamped visits to points of
interest (POIs), linking human mobility to activity categories such as dining,
leisure, and shopping. These traces support POI recommendation, urban analytics,
and simulation~\cite{feng2018deepmove,luo2021stan,yang2022getnext}, but releasing raw
traces exposes sensitive behavior. Synthetic mobility generation offers an
alternative data source~\cite{feng2020simulate,yuan2022activity,long2023synthetic,deng2025marionette}.

\paragraph{Relevance to the Web.}
We study a control problem for LBSN data generation: how to generate complete
check-in sequences under activity-order requirements while retaining the
population statistics used in web-data analysis. Scenario-specific synthetic
traces could support recommender stress tests and data augmentation without
releasing individual logs. The evaluated contribution is controllable generation;
downstream utility and formal privacy protection remain unevaluated.

A scenario may require leisure before dining or sightseeing before shopping.
Such precedence couples multiple, non-adjacent positions. A local condition
embedding does not ensure that every occurrence of one category precedes every
occurrence of another, or that both categories appear. Reordering completed
sequences can fix inversions but cannot supply missing activities. Road-topology
control~\cite{zhu2024controltraj} addresses geographic structure, whereas constrained
discrete diffusion (CDD)~\cite{cardei2025cdd} provides a general framework for
sampling under differentiable constraints. POI generation adds a further
difficulty: category tokens and POI identities can disagree, so controlling the
former need not control the actual activities of the emitted POIs.

We introduce \pcdg, a training-free control stack on the frozen Marionette
generator~\cite{deng2025marionette}. It adapts CDD's KL-anchored projection to
sample-level order and existence energies, with a distance-distribution
regularizer on POI logits. Category-consistent decoding then restricts POI support
using the sampled category. After diffusion, same-category geometry refinement
changes POI identities and coordinates while preserving actual categories,
times, and event order. This separates spatial refinement from the fields that
determine constraint satisfaction.

Across Istanbul and New York, the stack improves every constraint metric over
the retrained PO-CFG baseline. Geometry refinement lowers spatial divergences
without changing constraint metrics; the full stack improves all six fidelity
JSDs over the unconstrained backbone on Istanbul and four on New York. New York
CategoryTransition and G-RANK remain worse. These results identify a recoverable
spatial cost in this generator, rather than establishing that every
fidelity--control trade-off can be removed.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em,itemsep=2pt,topsep=2pt]
  \item We formulate category-precedence control for complete POI check-in
  sequences using order and existence energies (\S\ref{sec:problem}).
  \item We develop a training-free stack combining logit projection,
  category-consistent decoding, and constraint-neutral same-category geometry
  refinement (\S\ref{sec:method}).
  \item We separate category-order control from spatial refinement and measure
  the resulting constraint--fidelity trade-offs, including residual category
  and popularity errors (\S\ref{sec:exp:tradeoff}).
  \item We provide a two-city, three-seed evaluation, projection ablations,
  and a stage-wise comparison under a fixed selection protocol
  (\S\ref{sec:experiments}).
\end{itemize}
