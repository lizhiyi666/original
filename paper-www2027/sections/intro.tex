\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Location-based social networks (LBSNs) connect users' online activities with their visits to physical places, producing time-stamped check-in sequences that support human mobility modeling and POI recommendations~\cite{feng2018deepmove,luo2021stan,yang2022getnext}. Generating realistic check-in sequences can support behavioral simulation and the evaluation of location-based Web services~\cite{feng2020simulate,yuan2022activity,long2023synthetic,deng2025marionette}.

Activities in check-in sequences can be represented by POI categories, such as sightseeing, shopping and dining~\cite{deng2025marionette}. Beyond statistical fidelity, applications such as itinerary planning and POI recommendations further demand control over the relative order of categories, e.g., sightseeing before shopping, while leaving the exact POIs and other categories unrestricted. We formulate this problem as partial-order-constrained POI check-in generation.

Existing mobility generators have made substantial progress in modeling spatiotemporal patterns through neural networks, point processes, and diffusion models~\cite{luo2021stan,yang2022getnext,chen2025sequentialdiffusion}. Conditional generation further enables control through mobility contexts. Marionette~\cite{deng2025marionette} incorporates global and partial mobility contexts into fine-grained trajectory generation. Geo-Llama~\cite{li2025geollama} generates check-in sequences with explicit visit constraints. ControlTraj~\cite{zhu2024controltraj} constrains the generated sequence with road topology and trip attributes. However, these forms of control do not provide an \emph{explicit mechanism for preserving user-specified category-level partial orders} in the generated check-in sequences. Post-generation offers a straightforward alternative, but it cannot introduce missing required categories and may disrupt spatial coherence. As illustrated in Figure~\ref{fig:postswap-pcdg}, rearranging visits while retaining their original POIs can introduce long inter-area travel. Generating a coherent sequence therefore requires coordinating category precedence with POI selection, rather than simply rearranging an already generated itinerary.

Partial-order-constrained generation presents three challenges. \textbf{First, user-specified partial orders need to be accommodated at generation time without retraining the model.} Users may request different combinations of partial-order relations that are unknown beforehand. Anticipating all such combinations in a fixed training scheme is impractical. \textbf{Second, constraint satisfaction requires both the presence and correct ordering of the specified categories}. Penalizing ordering violations alone admits trivial solutions that omit the required categories. \textbf{Third, satisfying partial-order constraints must be balanced with preserving realistic spatial patterns}. The required categories and their relative order vary the POI choices and may distort travel distances and visit patterns. Preserving both spatial fidelity and the partial-order constraints remains an open challenge.

In response to the above challenges, 
we introduce \pcdg, a training-free framework for partial-order-constrained POI check-in sequence generation. PCDG expresses category precedence through a cross-position energy and couples it with an existence energy that penalizes the absence of required categories. It incorporates these energies into inference-time projection, adapting the KL-anchored constrained optimization mechanism of CDD~\cite{cardei2025cdd} to guide the generation process. This joint formulation discourages both incorrect ordering and avoidance of the constrained categories. Category-consistent decoding then restricts POI selection according to the sampled activity categories, connecting category-level control to the emitted POIs. After diffusion, category-preserving spatial refinement adjusts the final POI selection within the same categories, allowing spatially coherent generation while preserving the category constraints. Experiments on Istanbul and New York demonstrate substantial improvements in constraint satisfaction without additional parameter training. PCDG reduces the strict ordering-violation score from 92.89\% to 4.04\% on Istanbul and from 89.45\% to 11.28\% on New York.

Our contributions are threefold:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em,itemsep=2pt,topsep=2pt]
  \item To the best of our knowledge, we are the first to study partial-order-constrained POI check-in generation, where required activity categories must appear and satisfy specified precedence relations without prescribing a complete itinerary.
  \item We propose PCDG, a training-free framework that combines joint order-and-existence projection, category-consistent POI decoding, and category-preserving spatial refinement to coordinate activity-order control with POI selection.
  \item Experiments on two real-world LBSN datasets demonstrate substantial improvements in constraint satisfaction. Ablation and stage-wise analyses establish the complementary roles of the components and identify spatial discrepancies that can be reduced without altering the realized activity sequence. (\S\ref{sec:experiments}).
\end{itemize}
