\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Location-based social networks (LBSNs) connect users' online activities with
visits to physical places, producing time-stamped check-in sequences that
support human mobility modeling and POI recommendation~\cite{feng2018deepmove,luo2021stan,yang2022getnext}.
Generating realistic check-in sequences supports behavioral simulation and the
evaluation of location-based Web services~\cite{feng2020simulate,yuan2022activity,long2023synthetic,deng2025marionette}.
These synthetic traces also provide controllable inputs for Web applications
such as recommender stress tests and service simulation, where preserving
population-level mobility statistics is as important as satisfying a requested
activity pattern.
Relevance to the Web follows from this setting: the resulting traces support
controllable simulation and stress testing of location-based services while
avoiding the release of individual check-in logs.

Activities in check-in sequences can be represented by POI categories, such as
sightseeing, shopping, and dining~\cite{long2023synthetic,yuan2023stdpp,deng2025marionette}.
Beyond statistical fidelity, itinerary planning and personalized tour-package
generation require control over the relative order of activities. Users may
specify sightseeing before shopping while leaving dining flexible, rather than
prescribing a complete activity sequence. Partial orders capture these
requirements without fixing the entire itinerary or the specific POIs. We
therefore study partial-order-constrained POI check-in generation, where
required activity categories must appear in the specified relative order.

Existing mobility generators model spatiotemporal patterns through neural
networks, point processes, and diffusion models~\cite{li2025geollama,deng2025marionette}.
Marionette~\cite{deng2025marionette} incorporates global and partial mobility
contexts into fine-grained trajectory generation. Geo-Llama~\cite{li2025geollama}
generates check-in sequences with explicit visit constraints, while ControlTraj
uses road topology and trip attributes to control trajectory generation~\cite{zhu2024controltraj}.
However, these forms of control do not provide an explicit mechanism for
preserving user-specified category-level partial orders in generated check-in
sequences. Post-generation reordering offers a straightforward alternative,
but it cannot introduce missing required categories and may disrupt spatial
coherence. As Figure~\ref{fig:postswap-pcdg} illustrates, retaining sampled
POIs while changing visit order can create long inter-area travel. Coherent
generation therefore requires coordinating category precedence with POI
selection, rather than rearranging an already generated itinerary.

Partial-order-constrained generation presents three challenges. \textbf{First,
user-specified partial orders must be accommodated at generation time without
retraining for each order.} Users may request different combinations of
relations that are unknown beforehand, making it impractical to anticipate all
orders in a fixed training scheme. \textbf{Second, constraint satisfaction
requires both the presence and correct ordering of the specified categories.}
An ordering penalty alone admits trivial solutions that omit required
categories. \textbf{Third, satisfying partial orders must preserve realistic
spatial patterns.} Required categories and their relative order can change POI
choices, travel distances, and visit patterns, so constraint satisfaction and
spatial fidelity must be coordinated.

In response to these challenges, we introduce Partial-Order Controllable
Diffusion Generation (\pcdg), a training-free framework for partial-order-
constrained POI check-in generation with a frozen conditional diffusion
generator. PCDG expresses category precedence through a cross-position energy
and couples it with an existence energy that penalizes missing categories.
Sampling-time projection incorporates these energies into KL-anchored
constrained optimization, discouraging both incorrect ordering and omission.
Category-consistent decoding restricts supported POI choices to the sampled
activity categories, connecting category-level control to the emitted POIs.
After diffusion, category-preserving spatial refinement reselects POIs within
their realized categories, improving spatial fidelity while leaving the
realized category sequence and its constraints unchanged. On Istanbul and New
York, PCDG reduces the mean fraction of required category pairs that are
missing or inverted, counting missing categories as violations, from $92.89\%$
to $4.04\%$ and from $89.45\%$ to $11.28\%$, respectively, without parameter
retraining.

Our contributions are threefold:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em,itemsep=2pt,topsep=2pt]
  \item We formulate partial-order-constrained POI check-in generation, in which required activity categories must appear in a specified relative order without prescribing a complete itinerary.
  \item We propose PCDG, a training-free framework that combines joint order-and-existence projection, category-consistent POI decoding, and category-preserving spatial refinement to coordinate activity-order control with POI selection.
  \item Experiments on two real-world LBSN datasets demonstrate improved constraint satisfaction. Ablation and stage-wise analyses identify the complementary roles of the components and spatial discrepancies that can be reduced without altering the realized activity sequence.
\end{itemize}
