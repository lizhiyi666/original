\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Location-based social networks (LBSNs) link online activity to visits to physical places, producing time-stamped check-in sequences for mobility modeling and POI recommendation~\cite{feng2018deepmove,luo2021stan,yang2022getnext}. Realistic synthetic sequences support behavioral simulation and evaluation of location-based Web services~\cite{feng2020simulate,yuan2022activity,long2023synthetic,deng2025marionette}.

\paragraph{Relevance to the Web.}
We control the semantic order of synthetic LBSN visits while preserving
population-level mobility statistics, connecting controllable Web-data
generation to the Web Mining, Multimedia and Multilingual Content Analysis
track and simulation of location-based services.

POI categories represent activities such as sightseeing, shopping and dining~\cite{deng2025marionette}. Itinerary planning and POI recommendation also require relative category order, such as sightseeing before shopping, while leaving exact POIs and other categories unrestricted. We formulate this as partial-order-constrained POI check-in generation.

Neural, point-process and diffusion generators model spatiotemporal patterns~\cite{luo2021stan,yang2022getnext,chen2025sequentialdiffusion}. Conditional generation controls mobility context: Marionette~\cite{deng2025marionette} supports fine-grained generation and a frozen sampling-time backbone; Geo-Llama~\cite{li2025geollama} imposes visit constraints; and ControlTraj~\cite{zhu2024controltraj} uses road topology and trip attributes. These methods lack an explicit mechanism for preserving user-specified category-level partial orders. Post-generation reordering cannot add missing categories and can disrupt spatial coherence: retaining POI identities while changing visit order can create long inter-area travel (Figure~\ref{fig:postswap-pcdg}). Coherent generation must coordinate category precedence with POI selection.

Partial-order-constrained generation has three challenges. User-specified relations vary, so accommodating them at generation time avoids retraining for each order. Satisfaction requires both category presence and correct order; an ordering penalty alone permits omission. Finally, constraints alter POI choices and can distort spatial patterns, requiring a balance between order and mobility fidelity.

In response to the above challenges, 
we introduce \pcdg, a training-free framework for partial-order-constrained POI check-in sequence generation. PCDG expresses category precedence through a cross-position energy and couples it with an existence energy that penalizes the absence of required categories. It incorporates these energies into sampling-time projection, guiding the generation process with a KL-anchored constrained optimization mechanism. This joint formulation discourages both incorrect ordering and avoidance of the constrained categories. Category-consistent decoding then restricts POI selection according to the sampled activity categories, connecting category-level control to the emitted POIs. After diffusion, category-preserving spatial refinement adjusts the final POI selection within the same categories, allowing spatially coherent generation while preserving the category constraints. Experiments on Istanbul and New York demonstrate substantial improvements in constraint satisfaction without additional parameter training. PCDG reduces the strict ordering-violation score from 92.89\% to 4.04\% on Istanbul and from 89.45\% to 11.28\% on New York.

Our contributions are threefold:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em,itemsep=2pt,topsep=2pt]
  \item To the best of our knowledge, we are the first to study partial-order-constrained POI check-in generation, where required activity categories must appear and satisfy specified precedence relations without prescribing a complete itinerary.
  \item We propose PCDG, a training-free framework that combines joint order-and-existence projection, category-consistent POI decoding, and category-preserving spatial refinement to coordinate activity-order control with POI selection.
  \item Experiments on two real-world LBSN datasets demonstrate substantial improvements in constraint satisfaction. Ablation and stage-wise analyses establish the complementary roles of the components and identify spatial discrepancies that can be reduced without altering the realized activity sequence. (\S\ref{sec:experiments}).
\end{itemize}
