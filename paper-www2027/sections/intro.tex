\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Location-based social networks (LBSNs) connect users' online activities with visits to physical places, producing time-stamped check-in sequences for human mobility modeling and POI recommendation~\cite{feng2018deepmove,luo2021stan,yang2022getnext}. Realistic synthetic sequences support behavioral simulation and evaluation of location-based Web services~\cite{feng2020simulate,yuan2022activity,long2023synthetic,deng2025marionette}.

\paragraph{Relevance to the Web.}
We control the semantic order of synthetic LBSN visits while preserving
population-level mobility statistics, connecting controllable Web-data
generation to the Web Mining, Multimedia and Multilingual Content Analysis
track and simulation of location-based services.

Activities in check-in sequences can be represented by POI categories, such as sightseeing, shopping, and dining~\cite{long2023synthetic,yuan2023stdpp,deng2025marionette}. Itinerary planning and personalized tour-package generation require relative activity order beyond statistical fidelity. Users may specify sightseeing before shopping while leaving dining flexible. Partial orders capture these requirements without fixing the full itinerary or exact POIs; we therefore study generation in which required categories appear in the specified relative order.

Neural networks, point processes, and diffusion models advance spatiotemporal mobility generation~\cite{li2025geollama,deng2025marionette}. Marionette~\cite{deng2025marionette} uses global and partial mobility contexts for fine-grained trajectories; Geo-Llama~\cite{li2025geollama} imposes visit constraints; and ControlTraj~\cite{zhu2024controltraj} uses road topology and trip attributes. These controls do not explicitly preserve category-level partial orders. Post-generation reordering cannot add missing categories and may disrupt spatial coherence: as Figure~\ref{fig:postswap-pcdg} illustrates, retaining sampled POIs while changing visit order can create long inter-area travel. Coherent generation must coordinate category precedence with POI selection.

Partial-order-constrained generation presents three challenges. \textbf{First, user-specified partial orders must be accommodated at generation time without retraining for each order.} \textbf{Second, satisfaction requires both category presence and correct ordering;} an ordering penalty alone permits omission. \textbf{Third, constraint satisfaction must preserve realistic spatial patterns,} since required categories and their order affect POI choices and travel distances.


In response, we introduce Partial-Order Controllable Diffusion Generation (\pcdg), a training-free framework for partial-order-constrained POI check-in generation with a frozen conditional diffusion generator. PCDG combines a cross-position precedence energy with an existence energy that penalizes missing categories. Sampling-time projection incorporates both into KL-anchored constrained optimization, discouraging incorrect ordering and omission. Category-consistent decoding restricts supported POI choices to sampled activity categories, linking category control to emitted POIs. After diffusion, category-preserving spatial refinement adjusts POIs within realized categories, improving spatial fidelity while leaving category constraints unchanged. On Istanbul and New York, PCDG reduces the strict ordering-violation rate from 92.89\% to 4.04\% and from 89.45\% to 11.28\%, respectively, without parameter retraining.

Our contributions are threefold:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em,itemsep=2pt,topsep=2pt]
  \item We formulate partial-order-constrained POI check-in generation, in which required activity categories must appear in a specified relative order without prescribing a complete itinerary.
  \item We propose PCDG, a training-free framework that combines joint order-and-existence projection, category-consistent POI decoding, and category-preserving spatial refinement to coordinate activity-order control with POI selection.
  \item Experiments on two real-world LBSN datasets show improved constraint satisfaction; ablation and stage-wise analyses identify complementary component roles and spatial discrepancies reducible without altering the realized activity sequence (\S\ref{sec:experiments}).
\end{itemize}
