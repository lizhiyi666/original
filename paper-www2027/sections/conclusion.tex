\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}
The two-city, three-sampling-seed study does not assess training variability or
statistical significance. Istanbul's historical backbone used a different
training batch size from New York; its full training provenance is unavailable.
PO-CFG uses scale $1$, without extrapolation tuning. The shared distance-KL
weight and batch-level objective give no per-sequence spatial guarantee.

Finite-budget projection and decoder fallback leave residual violations
(\S\ref{sec:method:decoding}). Same-category refinement cannot repair New York's
category-transition gap. Training-side calibration is not independent validation;
its failed distribution gates and override are detailed in
\S\ref{sec:exp:tradeoff} and Appendix~\ref{app:grid}.

Stage (d) transfers a fixed geometry configuration; (d)$\to$(e) changes three
factors jointly, precluding single-factor attribution. Complex partial-order
DAGs, conflict detection, adaptive scheduling, training-time order losses and
downstream recommendation utility remain open. Synthetic output has no formal
privacy guarantee; distributional similarity does not establish safe data release.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
\pcdg combines CDD-style projection, category-consistent decoding and same-category
refinement on frozen Marionette. It improves all five constraint metrics over
retrained PO-CFG in both cities, and all six fidelity JSDs versus JointGen on
Istanbul and four on New York. Refinement recovers spatial fidelity while
preserving constraint-relevant fields. This restricted separability leaves New
York's category-transition and popularity errors unresolved.
\label{end:main}

\section{Algorithm and Default Hyper-parameters}
\label{app:algo}
Projection uses nominal weights
$(w_{\mathrm{ord}},w_{\mathrm{ex}},w_{\mathrm{kl}},w_{\mathrm{dist}})=(1,5,1,1)$,
Gumbel temperature $3$, $10$ outer and $50$ inner iterations, SGD rate
$\eta=1$, and gradient-norm clip $10$. Dual variables initialize at $1$,
penalties grow by $2$ up to $10^3$, and the violation threshold is $\tau=0$
with multiplier-update tolerance $10^{-6}$. Early stopping is disabled.
In the batch implementation, token KL sums over vocabulary and positions for
active sequences and divides by the full batch size; order/existence penalties
sum over active relations and sequences without that division.
The ten controlled steps are $36,32,\ldots,4,0$, totaling $5{,}000$ inner
updates per active batch. Distance KL uses $8$ routes, top-$32$ candidates,
$32$ bins, and path temperature $1$.
Formal sampling uses batches of $64$ sequences in FP32 with TF32 disabled.

Geometry uses distance/radius/fidelity weights $(1,2,0.1)$, $8$ paths, up to
$32$ candidates, temperature $1$, Adam rate $0.05$, $200$ steps, and noise
refresh every $20$ steps. The full-stack weights are $(\rho,\lambda,\beta)=(0.5,0,1)$.
Algorithm~\ref{alg:pcdg} covers one controlled reverse step. Geometry acts once
after the reverse process; its timing qualification is in Appendix~\ref{app:cost}.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{\pcdg: one controlled reverse step}
\label{alg:pcdg}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require reverse-step logits $\mathbf z$, constraints $\Mmat$, position masks, training references
\State $\mathbf y\gets\operatorname{detach}(\mathbf z)$; initialize duals
\For{$o=1,\ldots,10$}
  \For{$j=1,\ldots,50$}
    \State Relax $\mathbf y$ with Gumbel-softmax
    \State Compute order/existence and distribution terms
    \State $\mathcal L\gets$ weighted objective in Eq.~\eqref{eq:balance}
    \State SGD on active rows of $\mathbf y$; clip gradients
  \EndFor
  \State Update duals from hard violations, Eq.~\eqref{eq:dual}
\EndFor
\State Sample category tokens; decode POIs with shared noise
\State \Return next discrete state (Eq.~\eqref{eq:ccdecode} and fallback)
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{Metric Definitions}
\label{app:metrics}
All constraint metrics use actual categories $\mathrm{cat}(l)$ of generated
POIs, not the sampled category tokens. For reference sequence $i$, let $R_i$
contain each distinct-category pair $(a,b)$ satisfying
$\max\operatorname{pos}_i(a)<\min\operatorname{pos}_i(b)$.
Let $P_i\subseteq R_i$ contain pairs whose two categories both occur in the
generated sequence; $C_i$ is the set of categories involved in $R_i$ and
$\widehat C_i$ the generated category set.
For a present pair, $v_i(a,b)$ is the number of inverted occurrence pairs
divided by $n_a n_b$.

\paragraph{Ordering and coverage.}
Strict OVR averages $v_i$ over $R_i$, assigning $1$ to missing-category pairs,
then averages over sequences with nonempty $R_i$. Skip OVR averages $v_i$
over $P_i$, then over sequences with nonempty $P_i$. In contrast, coverage
and Unsat pool counts:
\begin{align}
 \mathrm{PairCov}&=\frac{\sum_i|P_i|}{\sum_i|R_i|},\\
 \mathrm{CatCov}&=\frac{\sum_i|C_i\cap\widehat C_i|}{\sum_i|C_i|},\\
 \mathrm{Unsat}&=\frac{\sum_i\sum_{(a,b)\in R_i}
 \mathbf{1}[(a,b)\notin P_i\ \lor\ v_i(a,b)>0]}{\sum_i|R_i|}.
\end{align}
Thus Unsat counts unsatisfied \emph{relations}, not sequences; pair coverage
does not require correct order. Empty denominators are undefined, not zero;
undefined OVR sequences are omitted from their respective means.
Tables express these rates in percent.
The separate diagnostic \texttt{token\_strict\_ovr} uses category tokens.
Mismatch rate pools the event-wise disagreements between tokens and catalog
categories.

\paragraph{JSD and filtering.}
For count vectors $P,Q$, the implementation forms
$p=P/\sum P+\epsilon$, $q=Q/\sum Q+\epsilon$, $\epsilon=10^{-14}$, without a
second normalization, and evaluates
$\frac12\sum_jp_j\log(p_j/m_j)+\frac12\sum_jq_j\log(q_j/m_j)$,
$m=(p+q)/2$, using natural logarithms. A zero-total vector is undefined.
Distance, Radius, DailyLoc, and G-RANK retain sequences with at least two GPS
points, independently on each side; the two category metrics include shorter
sequences. Distance additionally requires the aligned generated/reference
first and last categories to match. This subset can vary by method, so the
reported Distance is not an unconditional whole-population divergence.

\paragraph{Six fidelity statistics.}
Distance is cumulative consecutive-point Haversine distance (Earth radius
$6371$ km). With arithmetic latitude/longitude centroid $\bar x$, Radius is
\[
  \mathrm{Radius}=\sqrt{n^{-1}\sum_j d_{\mathrm{Hav}}(x_j,\bar x)}.
\]
Its unit is $\sqrt{\mathrm{km}}$ and it
is \emph{not} root-mean-square radius of gyration.
DailyLoc counts distinct POI identities per sequence.
For these three histograms, edges are exactly
$\operatorname{arange}(0,M,M/100)$, where $M$ is the larger observed maximum.
This usually produces $99$ bins, omits $M$, drops values beyond the last edge,
and renormalizes retained mass. If $M=0$, the divergence is $0$; a zero
histogram otherwise yields an undefined value. These historical conventions
are preserved for comparability, not proposed as new estimators.

Category averages category-frequency JSDs equally over the $24$ hours.
CategoryTransition is the equal-weight mean of conditional next-category
JSDs over source categories with any outgoing transition on either side;
self-transitions are included and sequence boundaries excluded. A row/hour
empty on both sides contributes $0$; one empty on only one side contributes
$\ln2$. No events or no transitions, respectively, gives an undefined metric.
G-RANK retains each side's top-$1000$ visited POIs (all if fewer), aligns
their union by POI identity with zeros for missing retained IDs, and computes
JSD after normalizing retained mass. It compares identities, not rank positions.

\paragraph{Deterministic audit.}
Evaluation v2 uses an unstable frequency sort at the top-$1000$ cutoff.
The v3 audit breaks ties by POI id; identity alignment is present in both
versions. Across the $18$ formal audit metric sets (six full-stack outputs
and twelve reused baseline sets), the largest absolute G-RANK change is
$0.0032$; other statistics agree within $10^{-12}$.
The full-stack/backbone G-RANK comparison remains $0.1766<0.1892$ on
Istanbul and $0.1912>0.1628$ on New York under v3.
This audit does not replace any v2 table cell.
