\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}
The two-city, three-sampling-seed study assesses neither training variability nor
statistical significance. Istanbul's backbone used a different training batch
size from New York; full training provenance is unavailable.
PO-CFG uses scale $1$ without extrapolation tuning. The shared distance-KL
weight and batch-level objective provide no per-sequence spatial guarantee.

Finite-budget projection and decoder fallback leave residual violations
(\S\ref{sec:method:decoding}). Same-category refinement cannot repair New York's
category-transition gap. Training-side calibration is not independent validation;
its failed distribution gates and override are detailed in
\S\ref{sec:exp:tradeoff} and Appendix~\ref{app:grid}.

Stage (d) transfers a fixed geometry configuration; (d)$\to$(e) changes three
factors jointly, precluding single-factor attribution. Complex partial-order
DAGs, conflict detection, adaptive scheduling, training-time order losses and
downstream recommendation utility remain open. Synthetic output has no formal
privacy guarantee; distributional similarity does not establish safe data release.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
\pcdg combines sampling-time order-and-existence projection, category-consistent decoding and same-category spatial refinement on a frozen conditional generator. It outperforms retrained PO-CFG on all five
constraint metrics in both cities and JointGen on six fidelity JSDs in Istanbul
and four in New York. Refinement preserves constraint-relevant fields while
recovering spatial fidelity, but leaves New York's category-transition and
popularity errors unresolved.
