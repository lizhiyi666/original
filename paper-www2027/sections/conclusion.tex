\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}
The two-city, three-seed study does not measure training variability or
statistical significance. The cities use different backbone batch sizes, and
full training provenance is unavailable. PO-CFG uses scale $1$ without
extrapolation tuning; batch-level distance KL gives no per-sequence guarantee.
Finite-budget projection and decoder fallback leave residual violations, and
same-category refinement does not fix New York's category-transition gap.
Training-side calibration is not independent validation; its failed
distribution gates and override are detailed in \S\ref{sec:exp:tradeoff} and
Appendix~\ref{app:grid}. Stage (d) fixes geometry settings, while (d)$\to$(e)
jointly changes three factors. Complex order DAGs, conflict detection, adaptive
scheduling, training-time order losses, and recommendation utility remain open.
Synthetic output has no formal privacy guarantee; distributional similarity
does not establish safe release.

When the backbone assigns little mass to required categories, finite-budget
projection can leave relations unsatisfied. Decoding addresses category--POI
mismatch; category-preserving geometry cannot correct errors in the generated
category sequence.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
On a frozen generator, \pcdg combines order/existence projection,
category-consistent decoding, and same-category refinement to support new
category-order requests without retraining. It outperforms retrained PO-CFG on
all five constraint metrics in both cities and JointGen on six fidelity JSDs in
Istanbul and four in New York. Refinement restores spatial fidelity while
preserving constraints, but leaves New York's category-transition and
popularity errors unresolved and is restricted to realized categories.
