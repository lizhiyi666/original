\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}
Several limitations frame our claims honestly. (i) We evaluate two cities; the
Istanbul base model was trained historically with a different batch size than New
York and its full data provenance is not retroactively verifiable, so we do not
claim identical cross-city training conditions. (ii) The reported error bars are
sample standard deviations over three sampling seeds at a \emph{fixed} checkpoint;
they reflect sampling stochasticity, not training variability, and we run no
significance tests. (iii) PO-CFG is evaluated at guidance scale $1$; we do not claim an
extrapolation-tuned optimum. (iv) The distance-KL weight is fixed, not tuned per city,
and is a batch-level distribution-matching regularizer rather than a per-sample spatial
guarantee. (v) \pcdg reduces---but does not certify zero---constraint violations; it
reweights the sampling distribution and performs no hard candidate filtering.
(vi) \textbf{Two New York statistics remain above the unconstrained backbone}
(\emph{CategoryTransition} $0.0183$ vs.\ $0.0070$; \emph{G-RANK} $0.1914$ vs.\ $0.1612$),
so our claim is that the trade-off is largely, not entirely, removable; on Istanbul all
six divergences improve. (vii) The $\rho/\lambda/\beta$ configuration was selected on a
training-side calibration split that the frozen backbone had seen, which is an
engineering calibration rather than an independent validation set; no test metric
entered selection, but neither of the two three-seed-confirmed configurations cleared
all pre-specified calibration thresholds, and we report the held-out test result of the
top-ranked configuration as-is. (viii) The transition from the geometry refinement to
the full stack changes three things at once---$\rho$, $\beta$, and the composition of the
same-category candidate pool---so we do not attribute that difference to any single
factor. (ix) The geometry refinement is a migration of
an already-frozen configuration; its candidate pools are built from the training
reference and the backbone's own scores, but it is not a from-scratch generalization
study. (x) We do not yet report a downstream-utility study (e.g., training a next-POI
model on the synthetic data). Addressing complex partial-order DAGs (transitivity,
conflict detection), adaptive constraint scheduling, a training-time partial-order
objective, and downstream utility are natural directions for future work.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
We studied controllable synthetic generation of POI check-in sequences under
sample-level activity-category precedence constraints---a web-native,
privacy-relevant problem that single-step conditioning cannot address. We
introduced \pcdg, a training-free, three-stage sampling-time control stack that compiles
a partial-order matrix into differentiable order and existence energies, projects the
category-position logits of a frozen joint temporal\,+\,category\,+\,POI
discrete-diffusion generator via a KL-preserving augmented Lagrangian, removes residual
category--POI inconsistency by category-consistent decoding, and restores spatial
realism through a constraint-neutral, distribution-aware same-category geometry
refinement. Across two cities \pcdg delivers the strongest constraint satisfaction among
post-hoc, guidance, and even fully retrained conditional baselines while \emph{also}
improving fidelity over the unconstrained generator on all six divergences on one city
and four of six on the other---without retraining anything. Our stage-wise analysis
locates the fidelity cost of constrained sampling on the spatial/POI side and shows it
is largely an artifact of distribution-blind POI selection rather than a necessary price
of control. We hope the training-free, sampling-time recipe proves useful for
controllable generation of other structured web content.

\section{Algorithm and Default Hyper-parameters}

$w_{\mathrm{dist}}{=}1$; Gumbel temperature $T{=}3$; outer iterations $10$, inner
iterations $50$; SGD learning rate $\eta$, gradient clip $10$; multiplier growth
$\alpha{=}2$, $\mu_{\max}{=}10^3$, tolerance $\tau_{\mathrm{tol}}{=}0.25$; distance
routes $P{=}8$, candidates $\kappa{=}32$, bins $32$; applied over the last $40$
reverse steps every $4$ steps. Geometry refinement (\S\ref{sec:method:geometry}):

$\kappa_g{=}32$ candidates, temperature $1$, Adam learning rate $0.05$, $200$ steps,

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{\pcdg projected reverse step (one controlled step $s$)}
\label{alg:pcdg}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require backbone logits $\mathbf{z}$; constraints $\{(a,b)\}$ with $\mathbf{W}_A,\mathbf{W}_B$; masks; distance reference $H_{\mathrm{train}}$
\State $\mathbf{y}\gets \mathbf{z}$ (detached); initialize $\lambda,\mu$ for active rows
\For{outer $=1$ to $T_{\mathrm{out}}$}
  \For{inner $=1$ to $T_{\mathrm{in}}$}
    \State relax $\mathbf{y}$ to $\boldsymbol\pi$; compute $g^{\mathrm{ord}},g^{\mathrm{ex}}$ on category positions \Comment{Eq.~\eqref{eq:order}--\eqref{eq:exist}}
    \State $\mathcal{L}\gets w_{\mathrm{kl}}\dkl + \text{AL}(g^{\mathrm{ord}},g^{\mathrm{ex}};\lambda,\mu) + w_{\mathrm{dist}}\dkl(H_{\mathrm{train}}\|H_{\mathrm{proj}})$
    \State SGD step on $\mathbf{y}$ (clip; restrict to active rows)
  \EndFor
  \State evaluate hard violations $\hat\delta$; update $\lambda,\mu$ \Comment{Eq.~\eqref{eq:dual}}; early stop if satisfied
\EndFor
\State \Return joint categorical sample from projected $\mathbf{y}$, decoded category-first \Comment{Eq.~\eqref{eq:ccdecode}}
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{Distribution-Correction Grid and Selection}
\label{app:grid}
The distribution-correction stage has three weights. $\rho$ scales the existing
constraint loss inside the augmented-Lagrangian objective
(Eq.~\eqref{eq:auglag}); $\lambda$ weights a category-side distribution term---the mean
of an hour--category JSD and a conditional category-transition JSD against the training
reference pool; and $\beta$ weights a POI-side distribution term---the mean of an
identity-aligned top-$1000$ visitation JSD and a soft expected-\emph{DailyLoc} JSD---inside
the same-category geometry refinement objective. We screened the full grid
$\rho\in\{0.25,0.5,1\}\times\lambda\in\{0,1\}\times\beta\in\{0,0.1,1\}$ ($18$
configurations) on a single seed, retained at most two jointly feasible configurations
per city, and confirmed those over three seeds, all on a training-side calibration split
with $512$ conditions per city and seed. Configurations were ranked by the maximum, then
the mean, of the twelve per-city JSD/JointGen ratios, with the parameter tuple as the
final tie-break. The two configurations that reached confirmation were
$(\rho,\lambda,\beta)=(0.5,0,1)$ and $(0.5,0,0.1)$; the first was selected and is the
configuration reported throughout. Spatial results are cached by
$(\textit{city},\textit{pool},\textit{seed},\rho,\lambda)$ and reused across the three
$\beta$ refinements of each batch, so $\beta$ varies at fixed upstream randomness.
No test-set metric participated in screening, confirmation, ranking, candidate
construction, or the refinement objective. As stated in \S\ref{sec:limitations},
neither confirmed configuration cleared all pre-specified calibration thresholds: both
met the \emph{Distance}, \emph{Radius} and \emph{DailyLoc} ceilings in both cities but
not the \emph{CategoryTransition}, \emph{Category} and \emph{G-RANK} ceilings on that
calibration split. On the held-out test set the selected configuration then met all
eleven gates on Istanbul and nine of eleven on New York.

\paragraph{Two definitions that differ from common usage.} \emph{Radius} is computed as
$\sqrt{\operatorname{mean}(d(\cdot,\bar{c}))}$, the square root of the mean distance to
the arithmetic centroid, \emph{not} the standard root-mean-square radius of gyration; it
is therefore comparable across the rows of our tables but not directly against
differently defined radii in prior work. \emph{G-RANK} compares the visitation
distributions of the top-$1000$ most frequent POIs; because many POIs tie at the
truncation frequency, the membership of that top-$1000$ set is implementation-dependent
under an unstable sort. All tables in this paper use the archived evaluation protocol
(v2). We additionally recomputed a deterministic variant that sorts by count descending
then POI id ascending and aligns the generated and reference sets by identity before
normalization. The effect on \emph{G-RANK} is at most $0.0032$ in absolute value and
changes no ordering or conclusion: on Istanbul the full stack remains below the backbone
($0.1766$ vs.\ $0.1892$) and on New York it remains above it ($0.1912$ vs.\ $0.1628$).
All other statistics agree with the archived values to within $10^{-12}$.
