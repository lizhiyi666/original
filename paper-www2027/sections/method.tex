\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\pcdg controls a frozen generator at sampling time through logit projection,
category-consistent POI decoding, and terminal same-category geometry refinement.
Figure~\ref{fig:framework} combines the framework and a controlled reverse step;
$\rho,\lambda,\beta$ enter the projection and refinement objectives, rather than
forming a separate execution stage.

\subsection{Backbone: conditional joint discrete diffusion}
\label{sec:method:backbone}
We build on a conditional generator that models arrival times with a diffusion
point process and the discrete content (categories and POIs) with a conditional
discrete diffusion model, following the fine-grained trajectory generator of
\citet{deng2025marionette}. The temporal branch samples event times; the discrete
branch represents a sequence as a token string
$\mathbf{x}=[\textsc{start},\,k_{1:n},\,\textsc{sep},\,l_{1:n},\,\textsc{end}]$,
with a \emph{category mask} and a \emph{POI mask} identifying the two segments.
Categories and POIs use separate absorbing-state transition
matrices~\cite{austin2021d3pm}; a Transformer denoiser with an
$\mathbf{x}_0$-parameterization predicts, at each reverse step, per-position
logits $\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{V\times L}$ over the joint vocabulary of size $V$
across the $L$ positions, from which the posterior $p(\mathbf{x}_{s-1}\!\mid\!\mathbf{x}_s)$
and the next discrete state are sampled. \textbf{The backbone is used as-is and is
never retrained by \pcdg}; our contribution modifies only the per-step sampling
distribution $\mathbf{z}$.

\subsection{Partial-order constraints as differentiable energies}
\label{sec:method:energy}
Given the sample-level matrix $\Mmat$, we parse it into a set of pairwise
constraints $\{(a,b): \Mmat[a,b]>\tfrac12\}$. To make a constraint differentiable
in the logits, we relax the hard categorical choice at each position into a
distribution. With temperature $T$ and (optionally) Gumbel noise $\mathbf{g}_l$,
\begin{equation}
  \boldsymbol{\pi}_l = \mathrm{softmax}\big((\mathbf{z}_l + \mathbf{g}_l)/T\big)\in\Delta^{V-1},
  \qquad l=1,\dots,L .
\end{equation}
We restrict attention to category positions $\mathcal{P}_k$ (via the category
mask) and to the category slice of the vocabulary, writing
$\pi^{\mathrm{cat}}_l(a)$ for the relaxed probability that position
$l\in\mathcal{P}_k$ takes category $a\in\mathcal{C}$. For a constraint $(a,b)$ let
$P^A_l=\pi^{\mathrm{cat}}_l(a)$ and $P^B_l=\pi^{\mathrm{cat}}_l(b)$. We define two
energies:
\begin{align}
  g^{\mathrm{ord}}_{(a,b)} &= \sum_{l\in\mathcal{P}_k}
     \Big(\textstyle\sum_{l'<l} P^B_{l'}\Big)\, P^A_l,
     \label{eq:order}\\[2pt]
  g^{\mathrm{ex}}_{(a,b)}  &= \relu\!\Big(1-\textstyle\sum_{l} P^A_l\Big)
                           + \relu\!\Big(1-\textstyle\sum_{l} P^B_l\Big).
     \label{eq:exist}
\end{align}
The \emph{order energy}~\eqref{eq:order} is the expected number of inverted pairs,
i.e.\ the probability mass of a $b$ appearing \emph{before} an $a$; it is zero iff
no $b$ precedes any $a$. The \emph{existence energy}~\eqref{eq:exist} penalizes an
expected count below one for either constrained category, which prevents the model
from trivially satisfying ordering by \emph{omitting} $a$ or $b$. Both energies are
computed only over category positions; POI logits do not enter
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:order}--\eqref{eq:exist}.

\subsection{KL-preserving augmented-Lagrangian projection}
\label{sec:method:project}
At a controlled reverse step, we project the backbone logits to reduce constraint
violations while staying close to the model's own distribution. Let
$\mathbf{y}^{(0)}=\mathbf{z}$ be the detached backbone logits and $\mathbf{y}$ a
trainable copy. We solve, per sequence,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
  \min_{\mathbf{y}}\;&\dkl\!\big(\mathrm{softmax}(\mathbf{y})\,\big\|\,\mathrm{softmax}(\mathbf{y}^{(0)})\big)\\
  \text{s.t.}\;&g^{\mathrm{ord}}_{(a,b)}\le\tau,\quad g^{\mathrm{ex}}_{(a,b)}\le\tau
  \quad\forall(a,b),
\end{aligned}
  \label{eq:constrained}
\end{equation}
where the KL term anchors the projected distribution to the base model so that
fidelity is preserved away from the constrained categories. We optimize the
augmented-Lagrangian relaxation of~\eqref{eq:constrained}: with
$\delta^{\bullet}=\relu(g^{\bullet}-\tau)$,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
  \mathcal{L}(\mathbf{y}) =\; & w_{\mathrm{kl}}\,\dkl
   + \!\!\sum_{(a,b)}\! w_{\mathrm{ord}}\Big(\lambda^{\mathrm{ord}}\delta^{\mathrm{ord}}
     + \tfrac{1}{2}\mu^{\mathrm{ord}}(\delta^{\mathrm{ord}})^2\Big)\\
   & + \!\!\sum_{(a,b)}\! w_{\mathrm{ex}}\Big(\lambda^{\mathrm{ex}}\delta^{\mathrm{ex}}
     + \tfrac{1}{2}\mu^{\mathrm{ex}}(\delta^{\mathrm{ex}})^2\Big).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:auglag}
\end{equation}
An \emph{inner} loop takes projected-gradient steps on $\mathbf{y}$ (SGD with
learning rate $\eta$, gradient-norm clipping, updates restricted to positions of
constrained sequences). An \emph{outer} loop evaluates the \emph{hard} violations
$\hat{\delta}$ (from the $\arg\max$ one-hot assignment, not the relaxation) and
updates the dual variables,
\begin{equation}
  \lambda \leftarrow \lambda + \mu\,\hat{\delta},\qquad
  \mu \leftarrow \min(\alpha\,\mu,\;\mu_{\max})\ \text{if}\ \hat{\delta}>\tau_{\mathrm{tol}},
  \label{eq:dual}
\end{equation}
stopping early once $\max\hat{\delta}<\tau_{\mathrm{tol}}$. Evaluating the
multiplier update on hard violations lets the penalty grow precisely for the
sequences and constraints that remain unsatisfied, rather than applying a uniform
fixed penalty. Crucially, the projection reweights the sampling distribution
over category positions only; it performs \emph{no} POI candidate filtering and
imposes \emph{no} hard category$\rightarrow$POI consistency mask.

\subsection{Distance-distribution regularization}
\label{sec:method:distance}
Enforcing category order perturbs the category marginals and, indirectly, the
induced spatial statistics. To preserve geographic realism we add an independent
distance regularizer that matches the \emph{population} distribution of travel
length. From the training split only, we compute the cumulative great-circle
(Haversine) route length $d$ of every training sequence with at least two POIs and
summarize it as a soft histogram $H_{\mathrm{train}}$ over $\log(1+d)$ with fixed
Gaussian bins (the reference is content-hashed and cached, never stored in the
model). During projection, for each POI position we fix the top-$\kappa$ candidate
POIs under the backbone and draw $P$ routes with a straight-through
Gumbel-softmax (exact distance on the forward pass, a bilinear surrogate on the
backward pass), forming a differentiable route-length histogram
$H_{\mathrm{proj}}(\mathbf{y})$. We add the term
\begin{equation}
  w_{\mathrm{dist}}\,\dkl\!\big(H_{\mathrm{train}}\,\|\,H_{\mathrm{proj}}(\mathbf{y})\big)
  \label{eq:distance}
\end{equation}
to the objective~\eqref{eq:auglag}. Unlike the order/existence energies, this term
acts on \emph{POI-position} logits, and it is a batch-level distribution-matching
regularizer rather than a per-sample hard constraint; it is applied only to
sequences with at least two POIs and uses an independent random stream. Its weight
is not tuned per city and is not guaranteed to improve every fidelity metric; we
quantify its effect via the \textsc{No Distance KL} ablation
(\S\ref{sec:experiments}).

\subsection{Placement in the reverse process and joint sampling}
\label{sec:method:placement}
The control is applied in the \emph{late} reverse-diffusion steps---where the
predicted categories are informative enough for the energies to be
meaningful---specifically during the last $K$ steps and every $f$ steps therein.
At each such step, \pcdg replaces the backbone category-position logits with their
projected values (and, when enabled, the distance-adjusted POI-position logits),
and the next discrete state is obtained by the backbone's ordinary joint
categorical sampling over the full token sequence, optionally through the
category-consistent decoder of \S\ref{sec:method:decoding}. The temporal branch is
unchanged. Because every operation reads and rewrites only the per-step sampling
logits of a frozen model, \pcdg adds no trainable parameters and requires no
retraining, and it reduces to the unconstrained backbone when the projection is
disabled. Algorithm pseudocode and default hyper-parameters are given in
Appendix~\ref{app:algo} and Figure~\ref{fig:framework}.

\subsection{Category-consistent POI decoding}
\label{sec:method:decoding}
The projection of \S\ref{sec:method:project} acts on category positions, but the
backbone emits categories and POIs as separate segments of the same token string and
samples every position from its own categorical. Nothing ties the POI emitted at
position $l$ to the category sampled at position $l$. This matters because the
constraint metrics are evaluated on the \emph{actual} category of each generated POI,
$\mathrm{cat}(\cdot)$ from the POI catalog, not on the category tokens: a sequence whose
category tokens are perfectly ordered still violates $\Mmat$ if the POIs realize a
different category sequence. On a $128$-sequence single-seed diagnostic (not one of the
formal runs) the projected backbone left $17.5\%$ of positions category--POI
inconsistent; enabling the decoder below drove that rate to $0$ and, \emph{without
altering a single category token}, cut the strict violation rate from $27.0\%$ to
$2.2\%$---so almost all of those violations came from the POI segment disagreeing with an
already correctly ordered category segment.

We therefore decode the two segments \emph{sequentially within one step} rather than
independently. Let $\mathbf{g}$ be the Gumbel draw used by the backbone's categorical
sampler at this step. We first take the ordinary sample
$\hat{x}_l=\arg\max_v(\mathbf{g}+\mathbf{z})_{lv}$, which fixes the realized category at
every category position. We then set, at each POI position (the set
$\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{poi}}$ given by the POI mask) the logits of every POI
whose catalog category differs from that realized category to $-\infty$ and re-take the
arg-max \emph{reusing the same draw} $\mathbf{g}$:
\begin{equation}
  \hat{x}^{\,\mathrm{cc}}_l=\arg\max_v\big(\mathbf{g}+\widetilde{\mathbf{z}}\big)_{lv},
  \quad
  \widetilde{z}_{lv}=\begin{cases}-\infty & l\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{poi}},\ \mathrm{cat}(v)\neq \hat{c}_l\\ z_{lv}&\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}
  \label{eq:ccdecode}
\end{equation}
where $\hat{c}_l$ is the category realized for the event at position $l$. Three
properties follow. (i) Category positions are never rewritten, so the realized category
sequence---and hence the ordering the projection just enforced---is preserved exactly.
(ii) Exactly one draw is consumed per step, identical in shape and order to the
unmodified sampler, so the ``off'' and ``on'' runs are paired at matched randomness and
no extra global, spatial, projection or distance randomness is used. (iii) Masking uses
true $-\infty$ rather than a large finite penalty, which would let a low-scoring legal
POI tie with an illegal one. At intermediate reverse steps the POI mask state is still
admissible and no restriction is applied when the category is itself masked or no legal
POI exists; at the final step only same-category POIs are legal and the sampler raises
rather than silently emitting an inconsistent token. The decoder adds no parameters and
no logit prior---it is a restriction on realized tokens.

\subsection{Terminal same-category geometry refinement}
\label{sec:method:geometry}
The stages so far decide \emph{which} category and \emph{which} POI identity; the
coordinates are then whatever the chosen POIs happen to be, and the spatial marginals
drift as a result (\S\ref{sec:exp:tradeoff}). After the reverse process completes, we
therefore re-select each POI \emph{within its own realized category}.

For every POI position we build a candidate set of at most $\kappa_g{=}32$ POIs sharing
the realized category: the originally sampled POI, the backbone's highest-scoring
candidates, the POIs closest to the generated trajectory's own centroid, and---in the
distribution-corrected variant---the most frequently visited POIs of the training
reference; duplicates are removed, the set is filled by model score, and ties are broken
by catalog id so the construction is deterministic. Scores come from one frozen,
untruncated $t{=}0$ evaluation of the backbone; reference GPS from the test split never
enters. Writing $\mathbf{w}$ for a relaxed assignment over candidates, we minimize
\begin{equation}
  \dkl\!\big(q\,\|\,\mathbf{w}\big)\,w_{\mathrm{fid}}
  + w_{\mathrm{d}}\,\mathrm{JSD}\big(H^{\mathrm{dist}}_{\mathrm{train}},H^{\mathrm{dist}}(\mathbf{w})\big)
  + w_{\mathrm{r}}\,\mathrm{JSD}\big(H^{\mathrm{rad}}_{\mathrm{train}},H^{\mathrm{rad}}(\mathbf{w})\big),
  \label{eq:geometry}
\end{equation}
where the two soft histograms are differentiable in $\mathbf{w}$ and the proposal $q$
places half of its mass on the original POI and spreads the other half over the
candidates by model score, so the fidelity term penalizes drifting away from the
backbone's own preference. We optimize Eq.~\eqref{eq:geometry} with $P_g{=}8$
straight-through relaxed paths, temperature $1$, Adam at learning rate $0.05$, for a
prequalified budget of $200$ steps, refreshing an independent geometry noise stream
every $20$ steps. The output is a \emph{hard} assignment: among the original assignment
and the $8$ optimized paths we keep the arg-min of the same training-side objective.
Empty, single-point and single-candidate positions, and batches in which the base
projection never ran, are skipped.

\paragraph{Constraint neutrality.} Because the refinement substitutes only POIs of the
same realized category, together with their catalog coordinates, and leaves category
tokens, the realized category sequence, event times, sequence lengths, event order and
all conditions unchanged \emph{per event}, every constraint-relevant statistic is
invariant by construction: OVR\textsubscript{strict}, OVR\textsubscript{skip}, pair and
category coverage, Unsat, \emph{Category}, \emph{CategoryTransition} and the
category--POI mismatch rate. We verify this numerically on every test run, requiring
agreement within $10^{-12}$ rather than assuming it. The refinement can therefore only
move the spatial and popularity statistics---which is precisely why it can repay the
fidelity cost of the constraint stages without giving any of their gains back.

\subsection{Distribution correction}
\label{sec:method:balance}
Three scalars govern the balance between constraint strength and distributional
fidelity, all referenced to the \emph{training} reference pool only. First,
$\rho\in(0,1]$ scales the constraint term of Eq.~\eqref{eq:auglag}, so the projection
objective becomes
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
  w_{\mathrm{kl}}\,\dkl
  &+ \rho\!\sum_{(a,b)}\!\mathrm{AL}\big(g^{\mathrm{ord}},g^{\mathrm{ex}};\lambda^{\bullet},\mu^{\bullet}\big)\\
  &+ w_{\mathrm{dist}}\,\dkl\big(H_{\mathrm{train}}\|H_{\mathrm{proj}}\big)
   + \lambda\,\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{cat}} .
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:balance}
\end{equation}
Second, $\lambda\ge 0$ weights a category-side term
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{cat}}=\tfrac12\big(
    \mathrm{JSD}_{\mathrm{hour}\times\mathrm{cat}}
    +\mathrm{JSD}_{\mathrm{transition}}\big),
  \label{eq:catbalance}
\end{equation}
computed from \emph{expected} counts under the relaxed assignment---no Monte Carlo
draws, so no additional randomness is consumed---with self-transitions counted,
sequence boundaries excluded, and reference rows without support contributing zero.
Third, $\beta\ge 0$ adds to Eq.~\eqref{eq:geometry} a POI-side term
$\tfrac12(\mathrm{JSD}_{\mathrm{top}1000}+\mathrm{JSD}_{\mathrm{DailyLoc}})$,
where the first is an \emph{identity-aligned} top-$1000$ visitation divergence (the
generated and reference top-$1000$ sets are unioned by POI id before normalization, not
compared as sorted popularity curves) and the second a soft expected-\emph{DailyLoc}
divergence. Spatial optimization results are cached independently of $\beta$, so the
three $\beta$ settings of a batch are compared at identical upstream randomness.

The configuration used throughout is $\rho{=}0.5$, $\lambda{=}0$, $\beta{=}1$, selected
on a training-side calibration grid (Appendix~\ref{app:grid}). We retain $\lambda$ in the
formulation because it is the natural category-side counterpart of $\beta$; that the
selected configuration switches it \emph{off} is an empirical finding about where the
fidelity cost lives (\S\ref{sec:exp:tradeoff}), not a modelling assumption.
