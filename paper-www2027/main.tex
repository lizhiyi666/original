\begin{abstract}
Location-based social networks (LBSNs) are a core source of web-scale human-mobility
data, yet sharing raw check-in traces is privacy-sensitive, motivating realistic
synthetic generation. Beyond statistical realism, many downstream web applications need
control over the \emph{semantic order} of activities---e.g., leisure before dining---a
sample-level, cross-position requirement that single-step conditioning cannot express.
We present \pcdg, a \emph{training-free}, sampling-time projection layer for conditional
joint temporal\,+\,category/POI discrete-diffusion generators. From a sample-level
category partial-order matrix, \pcdg compiles differentiable order-violation and
existence energies and, in the late reverse-diffusion steps, projects \emph{only the
category-position logits} through a KL-preserving augmented-Lagrangian optimization; a
complementary distance-distribution KL term regularizes spatial travel length to preserve
geographic fidelity. The projected category logits are then jointly sampled with the
unaltered POI distribution. Across two cities under an out-of-distribution split and three
sampling seeds, \pcdg more than halves the constraint-violation rate and nearly triples
pairwise ordering coverage over the unconstrained base generator, surpassing energy
guidance, post-hoc reordering, and even a \emph{fully retrained} partial-order conditional
model---without any retraining of the base generator. Ablations isolate the role of each
energy term, the adaptive multipliers, and the distance regularizer, and we characterize
the resulting fidelity--constraint trade-off.
\end{abstract}
